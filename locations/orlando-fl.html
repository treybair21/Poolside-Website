The Orlando Market

Lake Nona, Winter Garden, and the Horizon West area around Winter Garden and Clermont have been the fastest-growing parts of the metro, with new subdivisions adding pool-equipped homes every year. Windermere and Dr. Phillips carry fewer accounts but bigger homes, often with larger pools and spa combinations that push monthly billing higher. Closer to downtown, older neighborhoods have established pools too, but growth there has slowed compared to the western and southern suburbs.

Why Orlando Works for Pool Routes

  • Near year-round use. Central Florida's climate keeps pools in regular use for most of the year, unlike markets with a hard seasonal cutoff.
  • Sustained suburban growth. New construction west and south of the city keeps adding accounts to the market.
  • A tourism-driven economy with steady population growth. Orlando has added residents consistently, which keeps demand for service businesses healthy.

What an Orlando Route Is Worth

The same national framework applies: 10 to 12 times monthly service revenue. Routes concentrated in fast-growing western suburbs, with strong autopay enrollment and documented customer tenure, tend to land toward the top of that range.

Buying a Pool Route in Orlando

Florida's humidity means slightly higher algae pressure than drier climates, so ask sellers about their chemical routine and equipment condition specifically. Hurricane season (June through November) is also worth discussing — ask how the route has weathered past storms and what the backup plan looks like for missed service days.
