Pool Routes for Sale in Dallas-Fort Worth, TX

Buy or sell a pool route in the DFW metro · Poolside Business Brokers

Dallas-Fort Worth is one of the largest pool service markets in the country, mostly because it's one of the largest metros, period. The catch is that "DFW" covers a lot of ground — a route that looks great on paper can turn into three hours of daily windshield time if the accounts aren't clustered right.

We work with both buyers and sellers across the metro. If you're looking to buy a route or sell one, the first thing we'll ask about is geography, because in DFW that matters as much as the revenue number.

The DFW Market, Broken Down

Plano, Frisco, McKinney, and Allen have led the metro's growth for years and are packed with subdivisions where pools come standard. That density makes them some of the most efficient territory for a route in the entire metro. Southlake, Colleyville, and parts of North Fort Worth carry fewer accounts per square mile, but the homes are bigger and the monthly billing is often higher.

Closer to the urban core, older neighborhoods around Lakewood and Preston Hollow have plenty of established pools too, but the routes there tend to be smaller and more scattered since the housing stock is older and less uniform.

Why DFW Keeps Attracting Buyers

  • Population growth that isn't slowing down. Collin and Denton counties have been among the fastest-growing in the country, and new subdivisions keep adding pool accounts every year.
  • A long, hot service season. North Texas summers run from May through September at full demand, with light year-round maintenance in between.
  • Room to scale. The sheer size of the metro means a route that's maxed out in one suburb can usually expand into the next one over.

What a DFW Route Is Worth

Same national framework applies: 10 to 12 times monthly service revenue. What pushes a DFW route toward the top of that range is almost always geography — a tightly packed route in Frisco is worth more per account than a scattered one covering Plano, Arlington, and Fort Worth, even at the same total revenue.

  • Accounts concentrated in one or two suburbs, not spread across the metro
  • High autopay enrollment and clean payment history
  • Add-on chemical and repair revenue
  • Documented customer tenure of two years or more

Buying a Pool Route in DFW

Before you buy, map the actual stops. A 50-account route sounds identical on a spreadsheet whether it's packed into a five-mile radius or strung out across three suburbs, but the daily reality of running it is completely different. Ask for a service map, not just a customer list.

Financing follows the usual pattern: cash, seller financing (common under $150,000, typically 70-80% down with the balance over 6-24 months), or SBA loans for larger acquisitions.

Frequently Asked Questions: DFW Pool Routes

How much does a pool route sell for in Dallas-Fort Worth?

Typically 10 to 12 times monthly service revenue. An $8,000/month route usually lists between $80,000 and $96,000, with tightly clustered suburban routes at the higher end.

Is DFW a good market for buying a pool route?

Yes, especially in the fast-growing northern suburbs, though route geography deserves more attention here than in smaller metros.

What parts of DFW have the best pool route density?

Plano, Frisco, McKinney, and Allen lead on density. Southlake and Colleyville carry fewer but higher-value accounts.

How do I sell my pool route in DFW?

Work with a broker who prices against real DFW sales and understands the metro's geography. We typically get qualified offers within about two weeks of listing.
