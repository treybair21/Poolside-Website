What the Austin Market Actually Looks Like

Austin isn't one market — it's several. North Austin, Cedar Park, Round Rock, and Pflugerville have grown fastest over the last ten years, and the subdivisions built there are dense enough that a route can cover 50+ accounts without much drive time. West Austin, Lakeway, and the Lake Travis area are a different story: fewer homes per street, bigger lots, and pools that often come with spa attachments, water features, or larger surface areas that push the monthly rate above the metro average.

South Austin and areas around Buda and Kyle sit in between — solid growth, moderate density, and a customer base that skews toward first-time pool owners who lean on their service tech for more than just chemicals.

Why Buyers Keep Looking at Austin

A few things make Austin routes attractive when they come up for sale:

  • A long service season. Pools here run essentially April through October at full demand, with many owners keeping up light maintenance through the mild winter.
  • Population growth that hasn't slowed. New subdivisions in Leander, Georgetown, and Manor keep adding pools to the market every year, which means organic account growth is realistic for a buyer who wants to expand.
  • A tech-heavy, higher-income customer base. A lot of Austin homeowners would rather pay for reliable service than deal with a pool themselves, and they tend to pay on time and stay on autopay.

What an Austin Route Is Worth

The math here is the same as everywhere else: routes sell for roughly 10 to 12 times monthly service revenue, with the multiple depending on how the route is put together. A few things that move an Austin route toward the top of that range:

  • Accounts clustered in a specific area (Cedar Park and Round Rock, for example) instead of scattered across the metro
  • Strong autopay enrollment and a low history of late payments
  • Add-on repair and chemical revenue on top of the base service fee
  • Customers who've been on the route for two or more years

For reference, we recently listed a 55-account route spanning West, North, and Northwest Austin — from Bee Cave to Liberty Hill to Pflugerville — priced at $168,000, or 12x its $14,000 in monthly service revenue, with additional repair income on top.

Buying a Pool Route in Austin

Buying gets you a running start: existing customers, an existing schedule, and real financials instead of a business plan. Before you commit to a specific Austin route, it's worth checking a few things beyond the price tag:

  • Where the accounts actually are. A 40-account route that's tightly packed in Round Rock will run very differently than 40 accounts scattered from Manor to Dripping Springs.
  • How the seller collects payment. Higher autopay percentages mean fewer collection headaches for you after closing.
  • What the truck and equipment look like. Texas summers are hard on vehicles — ask when the truck was last serviced.

Most buyers here finance with a mix of cash, seller financing, or an SBA loan for larger purchases. Seller financing is common on routes under $150,000, usually with a majority paid at close and the rest over 6-24 months.

Frequently Asked Questions: Austin Pool Routes

How much does a pool route sell for in Austin, TX?

Generally 10 to 12 times monthly service revenue, the same national framework used everywhere. A $10,000/month route typically lists between $100,000 and $120,000, with West Austin and Lake Travis routes often at the higher end.

Is Austin a good market for buying a pool route?

Yes. Steady population growth, a long hot season, and a customer base that prefers hiring out pool maintenance all work in a buyer's favor here.

What parts of Austin have the most pool routes?

North Austin, Cedar Park, Round Rock, and Pflugerville lead on density. West Austin and Lakeway carry fewer but higher-billing accounts.

How do I sell my pool route in Austin?
